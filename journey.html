The art of sacred preparation

The Dieta: getting your mind, body, and spirit ready.

The Dieta nourishes your body, calms your nervous system, and clears away distractions. Sacred medicine often brings up whatever is ready to be seen, felt, and released. Preparing with care helps you meet it. You don't have to do it perfectly. What matters is the care you bring.

7 to 14 days before

Eat simply and cleanly

Cut back on or avoid: red meat, pork, processed or fried foods, extra sugar, caffeine and stimulants, dairy, alcohol, and recreational substances.

Enjoy instead: organic fruits and vegetables, light grains like quinoa or brown rice, herbal teas, and clean filtered water.

In the weeks before

Quiet the noise

Spend less time on screens, social media, news, violent or upsetting media, and outside drama. Make room for stillness: nature, silence, meditation, prayer, breathwork, and journaling.

Start reflecting on your intentions, emotions, fears, and desires. Notice where you want healing or clarity. Gentle movement, stretching, and cleansing with sage, palo santo, or sound can help too.

The day before

Rest and set your intention

Eat lightly, drink plenty of water, and go to bed early. Keep things calm and set clear intentions for your journey. Many guests choose fruit, broth, or herbal tea, and skip heavy meals for about 2 to 4 hours before ceremony unless guided otherwise.

The days after

Be gentle with yourself

Keep eating clean, wholesome food. Rest, spend time in nature, journal, pray, meditate, take salt baths, and breathe. Give yourself room to feel and reflect.

For several days, avoid hot saunas, too much heat, and hard exercise. Stay hydrated, but don't overdo water.

To keep the ceremonial space safe, recreational drugs and alcohol are not allowed anywhere at the sanctuary.

The art of integration

Beyond the ceremony. Into everyday life.

Integration means bringing what you discovered into the way you live, feel, and connect. Sometimes it happens in a deep conversation. Other times it unfolds quietly beneath the trees, beside the water, or in the simple company of an animal.

Soul Integration Coaching with Marla

Return to yourself, with or without ceremony.

Soul Integration Coaching is a guided way back to yourself. Together you build more honesty, self-trust, and emotional clarity. You explore the patterns, beliefs, old wounds, and survival habits that keep you far from your true self. This work doesn't try to fix you. It helps you live from what is already true inside you.

You don't need sacred medicine to begin. Many people come while moving through grief, a big life change, a relationship shift, recovery, or a spiritual awakening.

What sessions can include

  • Reflecting on recent experiences, including insights from ceremony
  • Seeing emotional and behavior patterns, triggers, and beliefs
  • Grounding, breath awareness, and nervous system awareness
  • Grief, boundaries, self-love, relationships, and spiritual awareness
  • Intention setting, personal accountability, and practical next steps

No session has to produce a breakthrough. Sometimes integration is simply noticing an old pattern and making one new choice. Marla won't tell you what your experience means or what to believe. She helps you find your own understanding.

Marla's story

She lived this before she taught it.

Childhood trauma, grief, and relationship pain shaped Marla's early life. In 2020 she faced serious physical and emotional challenges, along with the end of her marriage. What felt like her life coming apart became the start of rebuilding her relationship with herself.

Through years of study, lifestyle and nutrition changes, emotional and energy work, and spiritual practice, her life changed deeply. She released more than 60 pounds and found a new sense of purpose, self-love, and inner stability.

Holding space came naturally long before ceremony. As a hairstylist, she listened to people through grief, heartbreak, and new beginnings. Through her memorial jewelry business, Studiodragonfly19, she walked with families honoring loved ones they had lost.
