Potential, research & care

Sacred medicines. What are we learning?

You may be looking for relief, connection, or a new way forward. Explore what research suggests, what remains uncertain, and what to discuss with your healthcare team.

Research reviewed November 19, 2026.

Ibogaine5-MeO-DMTHuachumaSafety & care

Room for hope. Room for questions.

Researchers are exploring depression, anxiety, trauma symptoms, substance use, and some neurological conditions. A report about one person can raise a useful question. A study with a comparison group can tell us more about whether a treatment helps.

At Bufo Healing Sanctuary, we work with natural entheogens: sacred medicines used for spiritual exploration and connection. Our offerings include Bufo, Huachuma, and Iboga TA, held with preparation, ceremony, and integration.

Some studies below explore natural medicines. Others study isolated compounds or synthetic forms of compounds found in nature. We share this research to help you understand what is being explored. Their results do not establish what a ceremony at our sanctuary will do for you. We are not a medical clinic or a detox facility.

This page is education, not medical advice. No medicine is right for everyone. Research findings are not promises of healing, safety, or a cure.

Ibogaine & Iboga TA

Trauma, brain injury, and questions worth studying.

At the sanctuary, we work with Iboga TA, a plant-derived extract containing a range of iboga's natural alkaloids, including ibogaine. Much of the research below studies isolated ibogaine. Those findings help inform our understanding, but cannot automatically be applied to Iboga TA or our ceremonial offerings.

Observational research

TBI & PTSD

A 2024 study followed 30 male Special Operations veterans with mostly mild traumatic brain injury (TBI). After magnesium-ibogaine treatment and other supportive care, researchers found improvements in daily functioning, PTSD, depression, and anxiety through one month.

There was no comparison group. The study cannot show that ibogaine alone caused the changes or that it repaired brain injury.

A 2026 follow-up found continued improvements, with 25 veterans completing the 12-month assessment. Most also used other psychedelics or received other interventions during follow-up, making the results harder to interpret.

One published case report

Parkinson's disease

A case report described improvement in movement-related symptoms, fatigue, mood, and quality-of-life measures in one person after ibogaine treatment. A sleep measure worsened. This is an early signal that deserves further study.

One person's experience cannot establish that ibogaine treats Parkinson's, stops its progression, or regrows lost nerve cells. Controlled human trials are needed. Continue working with your neurologist.

Read the Parkinson's case report

Two published case reports

Multiple sclerosis (MS)

A 2025 report described two people with MS who received ibogaine. The authors reported changes in brain scans and improvements in symptoms. These observations invite more research into what may be happening.

With only two cases and no comparison group, this does not establish that ibogaine reverses MS, restores the protective coating around nerves, or prevents future disability. It should not replace your MS treatment or neurological care.

Read the MS case reports

Small observational studies

Opioid use & withdrawal

Small studies have reported less opioid withdrawal and reduced use for some people after ibogaine. Results vary, and uncontrolled studies cannot establish how it compares with proven addiction treatments.

A separate small follow-up study reported a death during treatment. Ibogaine carries serious risks. Our sanctuary does not provide medical detox, and this research is not a guide to managing withdrawal yourself.

Bufo / 5-MeO-DMT

New research into depression and anxiety.

Our Bufo ceremonies use a natural entheogen containing 5-MeO-DMT. The clinical trials below study a synthetic form of this compound, called mebufotenin. We include them as part of the growing research, while recognizing that they do not establish the same outcomes or safety for our natural medicine ceremonies.

Randomized controlled trial

Depression that has been hard to treat

A 2026 trial randomly assigned 81 adults with treatment-resistant depression to GH001, a synthetic 5-MeO-DMT product, or placebo. Depression scores improved more in the GH001 group by day eight. This supports further clinical research.

The controlled study period was short. Larger studies and longer follow-up are needed to understand lasting benefit and less common risks. These findings are not a guarantee for an individual person.

Read the depression trial

Early trial, no comparison group

Postpartum depression

A 2026 GH001 study involved 10 women with postpartum depression. All met the study's definition of remission by day eight, meaning their depression scores fell below a set threshold. The trial reported no serious adverse events.

This is encouraging, but the study was small, brief, and had no placebo group. It does not establish lasting recovery or safety during pregnancy or breastfeeding. If you are struggling after birth, seek care from your maternity or mental health team.

Read the postpartum depression trial

Self-reported survey findings

Anxiety & emotional well-being

In a 2019 survey of 362 people who had used 5-MeO-DMT in a group setting, many respondents with anxiety or depression reported improvement. Some reported no change or worsening.

The survey relied on people's memories and had no comparison group. It cannot establish that 5-MeO-DMT treats anxiety disorders. Anxiety and distress can also occur during these experiences.

Read the anxiety and depression survey

Huachuma / San Pedro

Connection, reflection, and early evidence.

Mescaline is a psychedelic compound found in Huachuma. A 2021 survey of 452 people who had used mescaline found reports of improvement in depression, anxiety, PTSD, and alcohol or drug problems among respondents who described having those conditions.

This was a survey about past experiences, not a treatment trial. It included different sources of mescaline and cannot establish that Huachuma ceremonies treat those conditions. Personal meaning and spiritual connection may matter deeply, while medical benefit still needs careful study.

Read the mescaline study

Your well-being comes first

Bring your questions. Keep your care team.

Ibogaine can disrupt the heart's electrical rhythm, with potentially fatal consequences. A monitored study found significant changes even in screened participants. A normal screening result does not remove this risk.

Read the cardiac safety study

  • Health history and medication review come first. Iboga screening includes an ECG/EKG and any further medical review that is needed.
  • Not everyone is a fit. Heart conditions, mental health history, pregnancy, breastfeeding, and other health concerns require individual professional review.
  • Never stop or change prescribed medication to attend a ceremony without your prescriber's guidance.
  • Research settings may use medical monitoring and support that differ from our sanctuary. Medical professionals are not routinely onsite here. Any additional medical support must be confirmed in advance.
  • Preparation and integration support reflection and ongoing care. They do not remove medical risks or replace evidence-based treatment.

If you live with Parkinson's, MS, brain injury, PTSD, or postpartum depression, bring the studies to your treating clinician. You deserve support that takes your whole situation seriously.
