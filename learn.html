Bufo Alvarius (5-MeO-DMT)

A naturally occurring entheogen known for expanded states of consciousness, emotional release, and spiritual insight. It acts mainly on the 5-HT1A and 5-HT2A serotonin receptors, which researchers believe may briefly interrupt rigid thought patterns and survival responses.

Emerging research and observational studies suggest it may support people facing PTSD, anxiety, depression, grief, or unresolved trauma, and some researchers think mystical-type experiences may support neuroplasticity when paired with integration. Because it can be intense, screening, trauma-informed support, and integration are essential. People with certain mental health or heart conditions, or taking SSRIs, MAOIs, or other psychiatric medications, may face higher risk.

Resource guide

Iboga TA: screening, kratom & preparation.

Everything we want you to understand before considering Iboga TA. How it works, why we screen so carefully, kratom and 7-OH, vitamin C research, and heart safety. Open any topic to read more.

For education only. This is not medical advice, and research findings are not guarantees of safety or outcome.

What is Iboga Total Alkaloid?

Iboga contains many natural alkaloids. Ibogaine is one of them. Iboga Total Alkaloid (Iboga TA) holds a broader spectrum of the plant's alkaloids, rather than isolated ibogaine hydrochloride (Ibogaine HCl).

We work with Iboga TA. We do not provide high-dose Ibogaine HCl flood treatments. Our approach is spiritual, ceremonial, and highly individualized. We are not a hospital, medical clinic, detox facility, or physician's office, and participation is never decided from a website questionnaire alone.

Human observational research has documented promising reductions in opioid withdrawal symptoms and cravings. These findings are encouraging, though individual responses vary and the research does not establish a guaranteed outcome.

Iboga, the brain and the nervous system

Ibogaine has an unusually complex profile. Research shows interactions with mu and kappa opioid receptors, NMDA and glutamate signaling, serotonin and dopamine systems, nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, and sigma receptors.

The body turns ibogaine into noribogaine, an active metabolite that stays longer and has its own effects. Research suggests meaningful noribogaine levels remain for days after a ceremony. Changes that last weeks or months should not be read as proof that it is still circulating.

People sometimes call this a "neurological reset." That language reflects an emerging area of science and should not be taken as a literal or universal outcome.

Kratom and concentrated 7-OH

Kratom (Mitragyna speciosa) is a Southeast Asian tree. Two of its most studied alkaloids, mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), act as partial mu-opioid receptor agonists. Regular use can lead to tolerance, dependence, and withdrawal.

Plain-leaf kratom and concentrated 7-OH are very different. Modern tablets, extracts, shots, films, and capsules can deliver far more 7-OH than traditional leaf. For screening, saying "I use kratom" is not enough. Please share the exact product or brand, its form, the amount, how often and how long, your highest recent use, taper history, other substances, the date and time of your last dose, and any current withdrawal symptoms.

Some programs give ibogaine soon after the last opioid or kratom dose as part of medical detox. That is not our model. We prefer that guests have completed their taper and have had meaningful time fully free of kratom or 7-OH. Timing is individual, and recent dependence may require consultant or medical review first. We do not prescribe, direct, or manage tapers.

Vitamin C and withdrawal research

Vitamin C has been studied for decades as possible support during opioid withdrawal. A frequently cited 2000 study (Evangelou and colleagues) reported lower withdrawal severity in people withdrawing from heroin who took high-dose vitamin C with vitamin E. Approaches such as the Schauss sodium ascorbate protocol are often discussed, and buffered forms may be easier on the stomach for some people.

These protocols are not standardized or first-line medical treatment. High doses can cause diarrhea and stomach upset and may carry added risk with kidney disease, kidney stones, or iron overload. The human research has focused on traditional opioids, so results cannot be assumed for kratom or 7-OH. We share education only. We do not prescribe supplementation or manage withdrawal, and vitamin C is no substitute for medical withdrawal care.

Hydration and electrolytes for heart safety

Ibogaine and noribogaine can prolong the QT interval and are associated with potentially serious heart rhythm problems. Low potassium, magnesium, or calcium can raise that risk. Withdrawal, dehydration, stomach illness, or poor eating can all shift electrolytes.

Magnesium supports nerve, muscle, and heart rhythm stability. Potassium is central to the heart's electrical reset between beats, and too much can also be dangerous. Sodium and fluids matter too: drinking large amounts of plain water without electrolytes can dilute blood sodium. Calcium also affects the QT interval.

Nourishing food, rest, and hydration help. Supplements are not right for everyone, and high-dose potassium or magnesium should never be taken without medical guidance. Normal lab results do not remove the heart risks of ibogaine.

How we screen before Iboga TA

We begin by getting to know you, not just your lab numbers.

  • Discovery Zoom. We meet face to face, talk about why you feel called, and see if continuing feels aligned.
  • Intake waiver and history. Medical history, medications, supplements, substance use (including kratom and 7-OH), past sacred medicine experiences, emotional history, and contraindications.
  • EKG or ECG. A recent 12-lead EKG, usually within three to six months, with QT and QTc, PR interval, QRS duration, rhythm, and a clinician's reading. A smartwatch rhythm strip is not enough. A urine drug screen may also be done.
  • Lab work as needed. Electrolytes, potassium, magnesium, calcium, liver and kidney function, blood count, and more, based on your history.
  • A complete substance list. Prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, psychiatric medicines, supplements, herbs, sleep aids, stimulants, recreational substances, and other sacred medicines, with dose, frequency, and last dose.

Never stop a prescribed medication just to attend a retreat. Medication changes belong with the professional who prescribes it. Screening is one part of deciding fit. It cannot guarantee that an adverse event will not happen.

What the research says, and what we never promise

Small observational studies report large drops in opioid withdrawal scores after ibogaine, and some people report less use and fewer cravings afterward. The evidence base is still small, mostly observational and open-label, with even less research on Iboga TA for kratom or 7-OH.

So we never promise a percentage of withdrawal relief, a guaranteed "reset," a set abstinence rate, or the end of cravings. Every person's body, history, preparation, and integration are different.

Iboga TA and Ibogaine HCl

Ibogaine HCl is an isolated form. Iboga TA holds a broader spectrum of the plant's alkaloids, and many people describe a different experiential character. Clinical trials showing TA is medically superior for kratom or 7-OH dependence are lacking, so we do not claim it is safer or more effective. Both can carry serious, potentially life-threatening heart risks. Iboga TA is simply the form of this medicine we choose to work with.

When something falls outside our scope, we may ask qualified consultants for review and request more documentation. Consultant review does not replace medical care, clinical monitoring, or emergency services.

This guide is for education. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, or detox guidance. Our intention is to share clear information so you can make an informed, sovereign choice.

Research study opportunity

Living with depression? Another path may be open.

Safety is one of our highest priorities. Sometimes medications, health history, or other factors mean ceremony is not safe or right for someone. When that happens, we help you explore other responsible options.

A clinical study in the Houston area is enrolling adults with Major Depressive Disorder. It is testing an investigational oral synthetic psilocybin-derived medication taken alongside a stable antidepressant.

It may be a fit if you

  • Have a Major Depressive Disorder diagnosis
  • Take a stable antidepressant and still have symptoms
  • Want to learn more about clinical research
  • Can attend in-person visits in Houston

Study support may include

  • Preparation sessions and two professionally supervised dosing sessions
  • Medical and psychological support
  • Integration sessions and follow-up visits
  • Compensation of up to $2,375 for qualified participants
